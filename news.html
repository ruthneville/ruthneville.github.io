UCL Public Policy Fellowship

London Borough of Newham · February–July 2026

In 2026, I spent six months as a UCL Public Policy Fellow working with Newham Council's Property team. My fellowship focused on how the borough's population is changing and what this might mean for future services and planning.

A major part of the work was developing profiles for Newham's 24 wards across eight neighbourhoods. These brought together evidence on population, deprivation, housing and local communities, using maps, charts and short summaries to make geographical differences easier to explore and discuss.

I also worked with colleagues on questions relating to stroke and adult social care, homelessness and temporary accommodation. A UCL Policy Fellowship micro-grant supported collaboration with designers and illustrators to explore more accessible ways of communicating the findings.

The fellowship was an opportunity to work directly with local government and think more carefully about what makes research useful outside universities, including the value of clear, accessible maps and concise briefings.
