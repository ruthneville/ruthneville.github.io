Research

My work covers international student mobility, migration and population mobility, and housing. Across these areas, I use spatial analysis, statistical modelling and computational methods to study how patterns vary between places and change over time.

International student mobility

Much of my research has focused on international student mobility to the UK. During my PhD at the University of Liverpool, I worked with UCAS application data to examine how international student flows are shaped by economic development, employment prospects, cultural and linguistic ties, and changes to migration policy.

This included research disentangling the effects of Brexit and COVID-19 on EU student applications. Using a difference-in-differences approach, we found a substantial decline in successful applications from EU students following the end of free movement, with effects varying by country of origin, subject and institution type.

More recently, I have used machine learning alongside time-series and spatial interaction models to forecast international undergraduate demand to 2030. This work identifies a concentration paradox: UK universities could become more dependent on a small number of origin countries even as application numbers stagnate or decline.

Migration and population mobility

Another strand of my work examines how migration and population movements respond to disruption, and whether changes persist once the immediate crisis has passed. I am particularly interested in what high-frequency and digital trace data can tell us about movements that are difficult to observe in conventional migration statistics.

With collaborators, I have investigated internal mobility during and after COVID-19 in Mexico and elsewhere in Latin America, as well as the potential settlement geography of people displaced from Ukraine. These projects combine digital data with more traditional geographical and demographic sources.

I am currently working on the uneven recovery of international migration after the pandemic, comparing the trajectories of migration corridors rather than treating recovery as a single global trend. This research is ongoing.

Housing and urban change

At the Bartlett Centre for Advanced Spatial Analysis (CASA), UCL, I work on the ESRC-funded No Space Like Home? project, which examines the geography of very small homes in England.

Using Energy Performance Certificate records and other administrative and spatial datasets, my work looks at where homes below minimum space standards are concentrated, how they vary across local housing markets, and what these patterns might tell us about housing development and planning policy.
